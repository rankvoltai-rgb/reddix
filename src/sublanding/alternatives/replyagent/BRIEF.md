# /alternatives/replyagent: brief

## Job

Someone searched "replyagent alternative" or "replyagent vs …". ReplyAgent is a hybrid: AI drafting, plus an optional service that posts from accounts it provides. The visitor needs to see which product they'd actually be buying, what it costs at their volume, and what Reddit's own rules say, then decide. Offer the Reddix trial.

## Concept: "Who posts the comment?"

The page turns on one question. The hero splits the frame into two lanes, "you post" (ReplyAgent manual mode, and Reddix always) and "their accounts post" (the posting service).

## Sections

1. Hero: a question H1 with the answer beside it; a two-lane band (surface vs ink).
2. Three products in one: manual mode, the AI plan, the posting service, each with its price; a line on Reddix.
3. Reddit's rules next to what the service says: vendor statements and Reddit's rules as two numbered lists, with no verdict.
4. Posting budget (interactive): comments, posts, expected removal %, AI plan (none/monthly/yearly) and a rate toggle for the two price sets its own page lists → a monthly breakdown with the 70% refund, what's still live, and a year next to Reddix.
5. When ReplyAgent is the better choice: a six-cell grid.
6. Where its pricing page disagrees with itself: four listed conflicts.
7. FAQ (sticky heading, open answers), Sources as chips, red closing band.

- ReplyAgent: RESEARCH.md §1–4 (active; replyagent.ai; AI plan $79/$699, 3-day trial, up to 3 products; manual mode with no subscription; $4/$8 posting from aged accounts 100–10k karma; 70% refund at the 24h/48h checks; optional approval or auto-posting; daily emails, Google-ranking priority, UTM, API), §4 conflicts ($5/$10 and "within 1 hour" in the FAQ; $7 vs $5.60; $90 vs $120), §7 (homepage "100% Undetected", "No account bans" unverified), §3 sample comment from u/tech_enthusiast_42.
