The best Syften alternatives depend on what you'd give up. For cheaper keyword alerts, look at KWatch.io or F5Bot Gold. For AI sorting across many platforms, Octolens. For scored Reddit leads, Subreddit Signals or Reddix. If you need Hacker News, GitHub and Slack communities in one feed, Syften is still the better buy.

Syften's own price page shows where the pressure starts. Entry holds 3 community filters, and its setup guide spends them on your brand, your domain and one competitor. AI filtering and Slack start $20 higher. X and YouTube add $60 more. This guide does that arithmetic first, then lists five tools in order, from raw keyword pings to lead qualification, so you can stop at the point that fits.

We make Reddix, and it sits at the far end of that list. It reads Reddit only and sends no alerts, so for many Syften users it's the wrong swap, and this post says when. Every price below was checked on November 3, 2026, on the vendor's own page.

## Key Takeaways

- **Syften's real prices** are $29.95, $49.95 and $119.95 a month, with a 14-day PRO trial and no free tier. Some pages still quote $19 to $79 and a free plan.
- **You're mostly paying for filter slots.** From Entry to PRO, the cost per filter falls 88%, from $9.98 to $1.20. The cost per daily result falls only 20%, from $0.30 to $0.24.
- **Entry's 3 community filters are the likeliest exit point**, by our inference. Brand, domain and one competitor fill them. No source we read states why people leave.
- **AI filtering and Slack cost a $20 step** up to Standard. With X and YouTube, Standard is $109.95 and PRO is $179.95, and X includes only 1,000 tweets a month.
- **The five picks run from pings to qualification:** KWatch.io ($19), F5Bot Gold ($49.99), Octolens ($199), Subreddit Signals ($29) and Reddix ($45).
- **The real decision is breadth or depth.** Syften names 17 communities plus the public web. Reddix reads Reddit only, scores posts against your product, and runs when you press Scan.
- **Stay on Syften** if you need Hacker News, GitHub or Slack community alerts. No Reddit-only tool replaces those.

### The Price of a Filter, and of a Daily Result

Two limits drive each plan: how many filters you may run, and how many results they may return a day. Divide the price by each and the ladder looks different. This is our arithmetic, using community limits only.

| Plan | Per community filter | Per daily community result | Daily results per filter, with every filter in use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Entry | $9.98 | $0.30 | 33 |
| Standard | $2.50 | $0.25 | 10 |
| PRO | $1.20 | $0.24 | 5 |

Move from Entry to PRO and a filter gets 88% cheaper, while a daily result gets just 20% cheaper. PRO gives you 33 times Entry's filters for 4 times the price, but only 5 times the results.

So the ladder sells query slots more than volume. That suits a team watching many narrow terms. A team with three busy terms pays for slots it won't use.

Standard's $2.50 is also a best case, because it assumes all 20 filters are live. As an illustration, a founder running 7 filters on Standard pays $7.14 a filter.

### The $20 Step That Buys AI Filtering and Slack

Entry includes neither. Both start at Standard, so their real price is the $20.00 monthly gap: $240 a year, and a 67% rise on Entry. They aren't sold apart from the rest of that step, which also brings 17 more community filters, 100 more daily results and 23 more archive days.

The AI part is narrower than the name suggests. You write [one rule per filter](https://syften.com/documentation), in the form "true if ...; false otherwise". A match passes or it doesn't, with no score. Rejected matches stay in the archive and API, so you can audit the rule.

It can work well. In [a test Syften ran and published](https://syften.com/blog/common-word-brand-monitoring/), 897 of 1,000 Hacker News comments containing "linear" had nothing to do with the product Linear. Phrase exclusions plus one AI rule kept all 103 real mentions and let one false positive through.

That's the vendor's own test, on one common-word brand, on Hacker News rather than Reddit. It also answers "is this our product?", which is a different question from "is this person ready to buy?"

## The Point Where You Outgrow Syften

No source we read states why people leave Syften. The pages offering reasons are selling the replacement. Everything in this section is our inference from its limits, not reported behaviour.

### The Fourth Filter

Entry's tagline is "Keep an eye out for your product name", and that's a fair description. [Syften's docs](https://syften.com/documentation) say "Add one filter per thing you want to watch". Its [Reddit setup guide](https://syften.com/reddit) asks for a filter each for your brand name, your domain and every competitor.

Do the count. Brand, domain and one competitor, and Entry is full. A second competitor needs a fourth line, because [Syften's blog](https://syften.com/blog/common-word-brand-monitoring/) says alternatives go on "separate filter lines". That leaves no room for any phrase that signals a buyer.

Its [buying-intent guide](https://syften.com/buying-intent-alerts) asks for more again: "Use separate filters for each phrase". The sample setup there has four lines, and the fourth uses an AI rule Entry doesn't include. So Syften's own recipe for finding buyers starts at $49.95, not $29.95.

The trial delays this moment. It runs on PRO, and [the homepage](https://syften.com/?redirect=false) says onboarding researches your company and creates your first setup. You may first meet the 3-filter limit on day 14, when you choose a paid plan. That's a reading of the page, since we haven't been through the flow.

### The Stream You Still Have to Read

Standard fixes the filter count, and 20 is a lot for a small team. It doesn't change the work. You write the filters and rules, read each alert, and write each reply. Syften is plain about this: "We provide the mentions and get out of the way."

If you like that, you haven't outgrown anything. If reading alerts has become the bottleneck, you've reached a job Syften isn't selling. That's the better reason to look at the lower half of this list, and price has little to do with it.

Syften has a fair answer to scoring tools. Its intent guide says it shows "what someone said, not an unexplained score". That line is aimed at account-level intent platforms, but the point carries. A score is one model's opinion, and the research for this post turned up no independent accuracy figure for any tool here, ours included.

### The Jump to Webhooks

Webhooks and MCP sit on PRO, a $70 jump from Standard. Check first whether you need push at all. The account API is on every plan, so a scheduled pull may do the job. If you do need push, KWatch.io offers webhooks at $79 and Octolens on every plan.

## Stay on Syften If

Switching has a cost. Keep Syften if any of these is true.

1. **You need alerts beyond Reddit.** Hacker News launches, GitHub issues, Slack communities. Reddix reads none of these, and neither does Subreddit Signals. Of the tools here, only Syften's site lists all three.
2. **Comments carry your mentions.** Syften matches Reddit posts and comments, while Reddix finds and scores posts. [Syften's blog](https://syften.com/blog/best-reddit-monitoring-tools/) says comments made up 87.4% of activity in the devtool-relevant subreddits it analysed. It gives no method for that number, and [the study it links to](https://syften.com/blog/best-time-to-post-on-reddit/) doesn't state it, so read it as a vendor's claim.
3. **You want to be told.** An alert comes to you, in Slack if you like. Reddix sends none and waits until you press Scan. For a support complaint or a brand mention, a push is worth more than a score.
4. **You've built on it.** The API is on every plan, with webhooks and MCP on PRO and webhook retries for up to 48 hours. If your own AI agent already reads and drafts, there's no reason to pay for those steps twice.
5. **Three filters cover your job.** Product name, domain, one competitor, email alerts, $29.95. If that's been enough, nothing below needs your attention.

## Five Syften Alternatives, From Keyword Pings to Lead Qualification

This order is a spectrum, not a ranking. Number 1 hands you a ping with a link. Number 5 hands you a scored thread and three drafts. Each step down adds more judgment from the tool, and usually gives up coverage, speed or money to get it.

To make the list, a tool had to be active on November 3, 2026, watch Reddit, and show a price on its own site.

### 1. KWatch.io — best for cheap keyword alerts that include LinkedIn and Facebook

KWatch.io is the closest thing here to a pure ping. You set keywords per platform and it sends alerts, with Reddit comments as an option you can switch on. [Its pricing page](https://kwatch.io/pricing), checked on November 3, 2026, counts keyword alerts by source.

| Plan | A month | Reddit | Hacker News | X | YouTube | Facebook | LinkedIn | Slack, webhooks, API |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Free | $0 | 2 | 2 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | No |
| Essential | $19 | 20 | 20 | 2 | 2 | 1 | 1 | No |
| Business | $79 | 100 | 100 | 10 | 10 | 5 | 5 | Yes |

Two things stand out against Syften. LinkedIn is on the list, where Syften's FAQ answers "No, unfortunately not." And X and YouTube come with the plan: KWatch.io Business with Slack is $79, while Syften Standard with X, YouTube and Slack is $109.95, or $30.95 more.

The trade is reach and judgment. That's six platforms, with no GitHub, Stack Exchange, Indie Hackers or podcasts listed. Its AI gives a sentiment label, so buying intent means tracking phrases like "alternative to" yourself. On speed, its pricing FAQ says "The refresh rate is per second" while [its docs](https://docs.kwatch.io/refresh-rates/) put Reddit in "Low Frequency (10 minutes to 1 hour)"; [our TrackReddit alternatives post](/blog/trackreddit-alternatives) goes into that.

### 2. F5Bot Gold — best for many keywords at Syften Standard's price

F5Bot Gold costs four cents more than Syften Standard. Here's what each buys, from [F5Bot's tiers page](https://f5bot.com/tiers) and Syften's own, both checked on November 3, 2026. The per-unit figures are our arithmetic.

| | Syften Standard, $49.95 | F5Bot Gold, $49.99 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Queries | 20 community filters | 200 keywords |
| Cost per query | $2.50 | $0.25 |
| Daily volume | 200 results | 5,000 alerts, 100 per keyword |
| Cost per daily unit | $0.25 | $0.01 |
| History | 30 days | 90 days |
| AI | A yes-or-no rule on any filter | 2 AI filters, with the first $10 of tokens a month included and the rest billed |
| Sources | 17 communities plus the web | Reddit, Hacker News, Lobsters |

Ten times the queries is the reason to look. The cost is coverage, and some precision: its FAQ says F5Bot "does not support regular expressions or wildcards", where Syften has a `*` wildcard.

One thing goes the other way. [F5Bot's AI filters](https://f5bot.com/docs-semantic) can read all new content in up to 5 subreddits per filter, with no keyword match first. Syften's rule runs only after a keyword matches.

Gold is the plan to look at for work, because Silver is labelled "Personal, non-commercial use." The rest of the ladder is in [our F5Bot post](/blog/f5bot-alternatives-reddit-monitoring).

### 3. Octolens — best for AI triage across many networks, at a much higher price

Octolens is the first tool here that sorts everything without being asked. [Its docs](https://octolens.com/docs/learn/ai-relevance.md) say its AI "reads every mention, scores its relevance to your business, and tags it", using a company description you fill in. Tags include Buy Intent, Competitor Mention and Product Question. You don't write a rule per filter.

[Pro](https://octolens.com/pricing) is $199 billed monthly, or $159 a month billed annually, for 10 keywords and 15,000 mentions. That's $19.90 a keyword against Syften's $1.20 to $9.98 a filter, though the units differ. X, LinkedIn, TikTok, GitHub and review sites all sit on Pro, with API, webhooks, MCP and unlimited seats. Syften PRO with X and YouTube is $179.95, only $19.05 less, for 100 filters.

Read these before you switch:

- **Quota:** the mention count [includes every post it pulls in](https://octolens.com/docs/learn/faqs.md), even those that fail the relevance filter. Overage on Pro is $0.013 a mention, and you can set a spend cap.
- **Sources:** podcasts, newsletters, news and the web need Scale, $599 a month, or $499 billed annually. Syften counts podcasts as a community on every plan.
- **Refresh:** its pages differ. The plan table shows Pro as "Hourly", while the docs call Reddit "real-time".
- **Judgment:** no numeric lead score is described, and Buy Intent isn't defined. Reply drafts come only through [skills your own AI agent runs](https://octolens.com/skills).

There's a 7-day trial with no card, capped at 5 keywords and 5,000 mentions.

### 4. Subreddit Signals — best for scored Reddit leads that come to you

Of the tools that score, this one beats Reddix on the point a Syften user will care about most: it keeps watching. It says matches arrive ["within about five minutes"](https://www.subredditsignals.com/reddit-keyword-alerts), by email on every plan and Slack on Pro. That figure is its own, not an outside measurement.

[Its model](https://www.subredditsignals.com/how-it-works/lead-scoring) scores each post from 1 to 10 on five dimensions: Lead Gen, Fit, Engagement Opportunity, Relevance and Authenticity. A lead is HOT at 7 or above on Lead Gen or Engagement. It calls Lead Gen "a prioritization score, not a conversion probability", a caveat that fits any score on this list. Drafts come on request, in your saved voice, and it "does not auto-post replies."

[The plans](https://www.subredditsignals.com/pricing), checked on November 3, 2026, are metered in a way Syften's aren't.

| Plan | A month | Billed annually | Brands | Subreddits | Weekly Lead Tokens | Cost per token, our arithmetic |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Starter | $29 | $24 | 1 | Up to 10 | 15 | About $0.45 |
| Pro | $59 | $49.17 | Up to 5 | Up to 25 | 25 | About $0.54 |

Tokens are "weekly credits you spend to unlock and act on leads", so a busy week can run out. It watches only the subreddits you pick, where Syften scans all of Reddit. Its pages differ on two points. Pro's card lists both "Up to 25 subreddits" and "Unlimited subreddits", while the FAQ rules out unlimited. And the scoring page says posts and comments, while the alerts page says only posts. It says it uses the official Reddit API. The 14-day trial needs a card.

### 5. Reddix — best for scoring Reddit threads against your product, when you choose to look

This one's ours, so the limits come first.

- **Reddit only.** No Hacker News, GitHub, X or anything else.
- **No alerts.** No email, Slack or webhooks, no scheduled scans and no background monitoring. If you don't press Scan, nothing runs. You get at most one scan every 5 minutes.
- **Posts, not comments.** It won't catch a brand mention deep in a thread the way Syften does.
- **No free plan.** $45 a month with a 7-day trial, or $150 once.
- **Data route.** It finds public posts through Firecrawl, a third-party search API, not Reddit's API.

What you get for that is less setup and more judgment. You paste your website, and Reddix builds a product profile and suggests subreddits. There's no filter syntax, because scans write their own buyer phrases from that profile. Each post gets [a score from 0 to 100](/features/intent-detection) against your product, in one of four bands: Hot, Warm, Research or Low.

Each thread then gets three drafts: Direct, Story and Expert. You edit one, click Copy & Go, and post from your own account. Reddix never posts, votes or messages, and uses no Reddit accounts. Leads you save go in a lead list with statuses for engaged, replied, converted and archived.

At $45, it's $4.95 a month under Syften Standard, and that's not the reason to pick it. Pick it if Reddit is where your buyers talk and you'd rather check on your own schedule than manage a stream. We publish no accuracy or results figures, so judge it on your own threads during the trial.

## How to Test a Switch Before You Cancel

Syften's docs call its `$accept` rule a Syften-specific operator, so expect to rebuild your setup in any new tool, not paste it. Give the test a full week.

1. **Count your live filters.** If it's three or fewer and email is enough, stay on Entry.
2. **Sort last month's matches by source.** Standard's archive reaches back 30 days, Entry's only 7. If Reddit isn't the largest share, drop the two Reddit-only picks now.
3. **Mark what earned a reply.** If most matches are junk, try one AI rule on Standard before paying to move. If most are useful and you're short of hours, the reading is your problem.
4. **Overlap, don't cut over.** KWatch.io has a free plan, Octolens a 7-day trial with no card, Subreddit Signals 14 days with a card, Reddix 7 days. Keep Syften running throughout.
5. **Compare threads you'd answer, not match counts.** Note which threads each tool surfaced that the other didn't.
6. **List what you'd lose.** Write down every non-Reddit match from that week. That list is the price of going Reddit-only.

## Where That Leaves a Syften User

Most people who go looking have hit one of two walls, by our reading, and each needs a different answer.

The first is a plan limit. If you've run out of Entry's 3 community filters, the cheapest fix is often Syften itself: $20 more buys 17 extra filters, AI filtering and Slack. KWatch.io and F5Bot Gold become real savings only when X, YouTube or keyword volume start to drive the bill. Octolens makes sense when you want AI to sort several networks and can pay $199 for it.

The second wall is the work. You have the alerts, and you don't have the hours to read them, judge them and write replies. More coverage won't help with that, though a scored list might. Subreddit Signals keeps watching and tells you when something lands. Reddix waits for you to press Scan, then gives you a ranked list and three drafts per thread.

Reddix is the wrong move if you need a Hacker News ping, a GitHub match or anything within a minute. It fits only if Reddit is where your buyers talk and you'd rather check on your own schedule. None of these tools comes with an independent accuracy figure that the research could turn up, so judge each on your own threads.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What are the top competitors to Syften?

It depends on which part of Syften you'd replace. For keyword alerts, the closest are KWatch.io and F5Bot Gold. For AI sorting across platforms, it's Octolens, and for scored Reddit leads, Subreddit Signals and Reddix. Directory lists also name PR suites such as CisionOne, which do a different job.

### Does Syften have a free plan?

No. Its page lists no free tier, only a 14-day trial on PRO with no credit card, then plans at $29.95, $49.95 and $119.95 a month, checked on November 3, 2026. Among the tools here, KWatch.io has a free plan with 2 Reddit and 2 Hacker News alerts. F5Bot also has a free tier, labelled for personal use.

### How much does Syften cost with X and YouTube?

It's $109.95 a month on Standard and $179.95 on PRO. Entry doesn't list either add-on. The X add-on includes 1,000 tweets a calendar month, with optional overage at $0.04 a tweet, so treat both totals as starting figures.

### Does Syften score leads or draft replies?

Neither is mentioned on its site as a built-in feature. Its AI filtering is a yes-or-no rule you write for each filter, on Standard and PRO. Its homepage shows a customer's own AI agent drafting replies from Syften's data. Subreddit Signals and Reddix both score and draft, on Reddit only.

### Should I replace Syften with Reddix?

Only if Reddit is where your buyers are and you're happy to press Scan instead of getting alerts. Reddix reads Reddit only and sends no email, Slack or webhook alerts. If you need Hacker News, GitHub or Slack community alerts, keep Syften. Some teams could run both, since the two do different jobs.
