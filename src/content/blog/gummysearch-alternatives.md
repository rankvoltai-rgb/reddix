There's no single GummySearch alternative, so replace it job by job. Start with Reddit Pro, which is free and run by Reddit. Then add one research tool: Karmit to find subreddits, Reddinbox for themes and questions, or PainOnSocial for cheap pain-point scans. Before you pay anyone, ask how they'll get Reddit data after March 2027. That question is what closed GummySearch.

GummySearch shut down on December 1, 2026, and its notice said all user data would be deleted that day. Its pricing page called it a "Reddit Audience Research Toolkit." Most replacement lists quietly swap that job for lead generation. This guide splits the old product into five jobs, names what covers each one now, and puts each vendor's own words on Reddit access beside it.

We make Reddix, a tool for finding leads on Reddit. It doesn't do audience research, so it isn't one of the seven. A short section after the list says where it fits. Reddix finds public posts through a third-party search API (Firecrawl), not Reddit's API, and we claim no licence. Prices were checked on November 3, 2026, on each vendor's own page. Features are as each site states them, and nothing here was tested.

## Key Takeaways

- GummySearch closed to new buyers on November 30, 2025, and shut down on December 1, 2026. It said it couldn't reach a commercial licence for Reddit's Data API.
- It did five jobs: subreddit discovery, themes and pain points, search, keyword tracking and saved audiences. No GummySearch alternative here covers all five.
- Reddit announced on September 30, 2026 that remaining public API access closes in March 2027. None of the 17 replacement lists we read on November 3 mentions that date.
- None of the six third-party tools in this list states a commercial Reddit licence. Reddit Pro is the seventh, and it's Reddit's own product.
- Pane, a listed alternative, said its Reddit intake would stop on November 13, 2026, because "the data terms that ended GummySearch reach us too."
- The cheapest setup we'd defend is Reddit Pro plus PainOnSocial Starter: $19 a month, $10 under GummySearch Starter's last listed price, by our arithmetic.
- No site here describes a match for saved audiences, so keep your subreddit lists in a file you own.

## Five Jobs Inside One Product

GummySearch's [product page](https://gummysearch.com/product/) billed it as "The #1 Audience Research tool for Reddit." Its [homepage](https://gummysearch.com/) named four uses: Ideate Startups, Validate Demand, Inspire Content and Find Sales Leads. Only the last is about leads. Under those uses sat five jobs:

1. **Subreddit discovery.** Find where a customer type gathers, across "130,000 active communities."
2. **Themes and pain points.** Sort talk into Pain Points, Solution Requests, Money Talk, Hot Discussions and Seeking Alternatives.
3. **Search.** Search one audience or all of Reddit, with filters and charts.
4. **Keyword tracking.** Get told about category requests, pain points, competitor complaints and brand mentions.
5. **Saved audiences.** Keep a group of subreddits per customer type, then work inside it.

The plan cards show what each job used to cost. Discovery sat on the free plan, with keyword searches capped at 50. Keyword tracking started at Starter, $29 a month. Advanced search started at Pro, $59. Those are the prices to beat.

[Trend Seeker's blog](https://trend-seeker.app/blog/gummysearch-alternative), which sells a rival product, says it plainly: "There is no exact replacement." We agree, so here's the map instead.

## How Lists Quietly Change the Job

A lead tool tells you who to reply to today. A research tool tells you what to build and what to say. Many pages ranking for this query sell the first and call it a replacement for the second.

Some are open about it. [Redreach](https://redreach.ai/blog/gummysearch-shutdown-alternative) calls itself "the leading GummySearch alternative" under a heading that reads "Built for Lead Generation, Not Just Research." That's a fair pitch if leads were your use. GummySearch's [own list](https://gummysearch.com/gummysearch-alternatives/) keeps the two apart: RedReach, ReplyAgent and SubredditSignals sit under "Reddit Lead Generation," while BuzzAbout, Adlicio, PainOnSocial and IdeaFast sit under "Cross-Platform Market Research" and "Pain Point Analysis." It also warns that "some of these might be affiliate links." [PainOnSocial says](https://painonsocial.com/gummysearch-alternative) its link there is one.

Others just miss. [G2's page](https://www.g2.com/products/gummysearch/competitors/alternatives) names Thinkific, a course builder, as the "best overall alternative." [Simular](https://www.simular.ai/alternatives/gummy-search-alternatives) says most former users combine two tools, and shows no data for it. In fact there's no reliable evidence of where they went. A Hacker News search shows no one saying, and Reddit's threads are blocked to our tools, so this post makes no such claim.

The best list we read is by [Spacestation Labs](https://spacestationlabs.ai/blog/gummysearch-alternatives), an agency that says it doesn't sell a tool. In October it counted 16 of Google's top 18 results as written by companies selling a replacement, so treat every small tool below as unproven. It also has a job-by-job table and calls Reddit Pro "the real successor." [RedNudge](https://rednudge.com/blog/best-gummysearch-alternatives-2026) ran a section in May on why every replacement faces the same risk. Credit to both. Neither quotes each vendor's position job by job, and neither mentions March 2027.

## The Reason It Closed Is Still Open

GummySearch's [closure notice](https://gummysearch.com/docs/articles/gummysearch-is-now-closed-6533h) blames "the inability to reach a commercial license agreement with Reddit's Data API." The founder's [closing post](https://gummysearch.com/closing-time/) says Reddit's policies "forbid commercial applications." Demand wasn't the issue: [Failory](https://newsletter.failory.com/p/when-reddit-pulls-over) reported a team of one and $35K in monthly revenue.

His [earlier post](https://gummysearch.com/final-chapter/) adds the line that matters for you. He says there are "many products that use the Reddit API without a commercial license," and that he didn't want to run a business "looking over your shoulder every day." He named nobody, and neither do we. But the question he couldn't get past now reaches every GummySearch alternative you might pick.

### What Reddit Announced on September 30

Reddit's own post is blocked to our tools, so these dates come from the press.

- **October 31, 2026:** new public API access requests stopped ([TechRepublic](https://www.techrepublic.com/article/news-reddit-rss-public-api-shutdown/), [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/379892/reddit-killing-rss-feeds-public-api-access-ai-scrapers)).
- **November 13, 2026:** RSS feeds were due to end ([heise](https://www.heise.de/en/news/Reddit-to-shut-down-RSS-feeds-and-public-API-11473400.html), TechRepublic, Decrypt).
- **January 12, 2027:** access starts closing for apps and users that haven't registered (Decrypt, TechRepublic).
- **March 2027:** remaining public API access closes (all three).

Reddit's stated reason is scraping and automated abuse. Reports differ on what registering buys you. heise says "Registered API users are not affected." TechRepublic says "Registration alone does not preserve legacy public API access indefinitely." [How2Shout](https://www.how2shout.com/news/reddit-rss-public-api-shutdown-dates.html) says social listening products will need a commercial agreement from March 2027. None of this says any named vendor will lose access, and we don't say so either.

### Why Pane Isn't on This List

Pane was going to be a pick. Then we read its [migration page](https://panegains.com/gummysearch-alternative), updated November 2: "The data terms that ended GummySearch reach us too." It says everything already in its database stays, over 1.2 million complaints at that date. New ones come from Hacker News, App Store reviews and forums. We left it out because a replacement for Reddit research should still read Reddit. But credit the note. It gives a date, says what stays and says what replaces it. That's the answer you want from every vendor here.

## Seven Research Tools, Ranked for This Job

To make the list, a GummySearch alternative had to do at least one research job on its own site, publish a price and show signs of life. The order reflects fit for research and how little it costs to find out if it suits you. Prices were checked on November 3, 2026.

### 1. Reddit Pro — best free start for discovery and keyword tracking

Start here, because it's the one GummySearch alternative with no access question. [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/01/07/reddit-intros-new-trends-tools-for-businesses-and-an-ama-ad-format/) calls it Reddit's "free suite of tools for brands." [Practical Ecommerce](https://www.practicalecommerce.com/seo-wins-from-reddit-pro) says it's free for businesses that give a name, URL, category and employee count, and that "Approval is instant."

Its Trends tab tracks a keyword and shows conversation volume, the communities mentioning it and a feed of conversations. TechCrunch's 2025 report limits this to around 100,000 "smart" keywords, while Practical Ecommerce's 2026 piece says "any topic or keyword." It also describes AI notes on context and sentiment, a weekly email, a Questions tab and competitor monitoring.

**The limits:** the chart filter "is limited to 3 prior months," and the Questions tab also stops at 3 months, so it won't show long trends. Vendor blog [BrandMentions](https://brandmentions.com/blog/best-social-listening-tools-reddit/) calls it a beta for "eligible businesses" and says it isn't a traditional sentiment tool, which sits oddly with Practical Ecommerce. [Adlicio](https://tryadlicio.com/blog/best-reddit-research-tools) says Trends data is for internal use only, which matters if you report to clients. No source we read describes saved subreddit groups.

Reddit's own pages are blocked to our tools, so all of this is second-hand. Confirm the details in the product. It isn't on GummySearch's own list or on 14 others we read. Only Spacestation Labs and Adlicio name it.

### 2. Karmit — best for finding and searching subreddits, if you accept a very new product

As a GummySearch alternative, [Karmit](https://karmit.co/) is the closest match to the old browsing habit, on our reading. Its free Community Map plots subreddits as dots, with "Neighbours by shared commenters, not keywords." Voice of Customer takes AND, OR and NOT keywords, a time range and subreddits, then reports pain points and sentiment tied to threads. Hotspot Analysis keeps a card per subreddit.

**Price:** the map and community search are free. Credit packs are one-time: $10 for 10,000 or $40 for 45,000, which don't expire. That's $1.00 per 1,000 credits against about $0.89, by our arithmetic. AI runs bill by tokens and agent time, and the page gives no typical cost, so start at $10.

**Unproven:** AlternativeTo [added it on October 14, 2026](https://alternativeto.net/software/karmit/about), and we'd treat it as untried. Monitoring is stated as every five minutes, a vendor target. Its pricing lists "Real-time keyword monitoring" under Custom, while its FAQ bills monitoring scans by credit, so ask which applies.

### 3. Reddinbox — best for asking a research question and getting themed answers

[Reddinbox](https://reddinbox.com/) is an AI research agent. You ask a question and it searches Reddit, X, Bluesky, Hacker News and Facebook, then sorts results into tabs: Top Pains, Questions, Trending and Vocabulary. On our reading, that's the nearest thing to the old theme buckets. Its free tools include a subreddit finder and subreddit stats.

**Price:** [Starter is $39 a month](https://reddinbox.com/pricing) for about 100 AI conversations and 3 Market Briefs. Pro is $99 for about 266 and 5. That's about $0.39 a conversation against $0.37, by our arithmetic, so Pro buys volume rather than a better rate. There's a free trial with no card.

**Worth knowing:** [its own GummySearch post](https://reddinbox.com/blog/best-gummysearch-alternative) ranks itself first, yet sends keyword alerts to Syften or F5Bot, even though its pricing lists monitoring.

### 4. PainOnSocial — best for cheap, repeatable pain-point scans

As a GummySearch alternative, [PainOnSocial](https://painonsocial.com/) does one job cheaply. You pick communities, it reads 7, 30 or 90 days of posts, and it ranks pain points by AI score with quotes and Reddit links. Its subreddit guides for 800+ professions help with discovery.

**Price:** [Starter is $19 a month](https://painonsocial.com/pricing) with a 7-day trial: 20 scans, 2 subreddits a scan and 1 weekly topic watch. Professional is $49: 300 scans, 5 subreddits a scan and 5 watches. By our arithmetic that's $0.95 a scan against about $0.16, so heavy users should skip Starter.

**On access,** its terms are the clearest of the seven, and they add that analysis uses "third-party APIs (Perplexity and OpenAI)." heise and Decrypt report that Reddit has sued Perplexity. The terms don't connect those two things and neither do we, but it's a fair thing to ask about.

### 5. IdeaFast — best for a one-time payment when you're testing an idea

[IdeaFast](https://www.ideafast.pro/) clusters recurring complaints, scores each 0 to 100 and links every one to its posts. It says its problems and quotes come from Reddit only. It's a GummySearch alternative for the "should I build this?" moment, not for ongoing tracking.

**Price:** the first scan is free, on 1 subreddit. Founder is $19 once for 40 scans at 2 subreddits each. Builder is $49 once for 100 scans at 5 each. Scans never expire, and there's a refund within 7 days if you've used 5 or fewer. That's about 48 cents against 49 cents a scan, so Builder buys wider scans, not cheaper ones. Forty scans on PainOnSocial Starter take two months and $38.

**Worth asking:** AlternativeTo [lists it as "Since 2026."](https://alternativeto.net/software/gummysearch/) [Its guide](https://www.ideafast.pro/blog/how-to-extract-reddit-data), updated October 24, says "RSS is almost always the right answer" for market research, without saying its own scans use RSS. Press reports put RSS's end at November 13, so ask what scans run on now. Its homepage demo showed posts from November 1 when we looked on November 3.

### 6. Adlicio — best when the research feeds ads and landing pages

[Adlicio](https://tryadlicio.com/) pulls comments and reviews from Reddit, Amazon, TikTok, YouTube, Trustpilot and more, then ranks the pains, objections and hooks in customers' own words. GummySearch's list files it under market research, but its pages speak to ad angles. The ones we read don't describe browsing or sizing communities.

**Price:** a [free plan](https://tryadlicio.com/pricing) gives 150 comments on your first scrape with no card. Starter is $49 a month for 250 credits, 3 brands and 1 Reddit Radar. Growth is $99 and Scale is $199. A Reddit topic run costs 1 credit per 5 threads, so 250 credits reach about 1,250 threads if you spend them only there, by our arithmetic.

If your research ends up in copy, it's a sensible GummySearch alternative. If it ends in a product decision, try the tools above first.

### 7. BuzzAbout — best for a funded team researching beyond Reddit

[BuzzAbout](https://buzzabout.ai/) runs AI agents over mentions from Reddit, YouTube, X, LinkedIn, TikTok, Instagram and Facebook, looking for pains, objections and questions. It's the priciest GummySearch alternative here, and the headline price understates that.

**Price:** [Starter is $98 a month](https://buzzabout.ai/pricing), but its plan card marks "Audience research," "Multi-source researches" and "CSV/PDF export" as not included. Those start at Pro, $178 a month, with 5,000 credits and 3 seats. Business is $450. That makes the audience research job about three times GummySearch Pro's $59, by our arithmetic. Eligible monthly sign-ups start with a $1 one-day trial.

If Reddit is one channel among several, the breadth may earn it. For a solo founder, it's hard to justify.

### Checked, but Not Picks

- **Subreddit Signals:** [$29 and $59 a month](https://www.subredditsignals.com/pricing). It's a lead tool first, and says its Pain Points Radar "is not exhaustive Reddit-wide trend analysis."
- **Trend Seeker:** [free, $9.99 and $39 a month](https://trend-seeker.app/pricing). It's built for finding business ideas, not studying an audience.
- **Peekdit and GigaBrain:** older lists name them. On November 3, peekdit.com returned a 404 and thegigabrain.com failed to load.

## Where Reddix Fits, and Where It Doesn't

Reddix isn't a research tool, so don't buy it as a GummySearch alternative for that job. It covers a slice of the old product: the Solution Requests and Seeking Alternatives themes, where someone is asking for a product like yours.

It finds Reddit posts and scores each 0 to 100 against your product (Hot, Warm, Research, Low), then drafts three replies per thread (Direct, Story, Expert). You edit, click Copy & Go, and post from your own account. Reddix never posts, votes or messages for you, and uses no Reddit accounts. Its [subreddit discovery](/features/subreddit-discovery) suggests communities from your product profile, to choose what to scan. You get a reason and an AI-estimated activity level, not verified member counts or growth charts.

**What it doesn't do:** themes, audience research, background monitoring, scheduled scans, or email, Slack and webhook alerts. Scans run when you press Scan, at most one every 5 minutes. It's Reddit only. Premium is $45 a month with a 7-day trial, or $150 once, and there's no free plan.

**Its data route:** public posts through Firecrawl, a third-party search API, not Reddit's API. We claim no licence and don't claim that route is immune to Reddit's changes.

## Four Questions Before You Pay for a GummySearch Alternative

[TechRepublic tells buyers](https://www.techrepublic.com/article/news-reddit-rss-public-api-shutdown/) to "ask vendors how they plan to maintain access after March 2027." We'd make it four questions, sent by email so you keep the reply:

1. **Where does your Reddit data come from today?** The official API, a data provider, public pages or feeds?
2. **Do you hold a commercial agreement with Reddit?** If so, will you say so in writing?
3. **What's your plan for March 2027?** A good answer names a date and a fallback, as Pane's did.
4. **If Reddit access ends, what happens to my plan and my data?** Ask about refunds, unused credits and export.

A vague answer isn't proof of trouble. But GummySearch's founder tried to solve this and failed, so you're allowed to ask a small vendor for a straight reply.

## Starting Over After the Deletion

GummySearch's notice said all user data would be "permanently deleted on December 1, 2026," and no recovery route is mentioned on its site. If you exported earlier, start from that file. If not, this order rebuilds the part that took longest:

1. **List what you remember.** Write down each customer type and every subreddit you can recall.
2. **Run your terms through Reddit Pro.** Add your category, brand and top competitor, then note the communities each one shows.
3. **Find the neighbours.** Open your best-known subreddit on Karmit's free map and check what sits beside it.
4. **Save it in a file you own.** Use four columns: subreddit, customer type, why it matters, date checked.
5. **Turn themes into search phrases.** As illustrations, not a tested list: "what do you use for [task]" for Solution Requests, "alternative to [competitor]" for Seeking Alternatives, and "worth paying for" for Money Talk.
6. **Check export before you commit.** PainOnSocial lists "Export results," and IdeaFast's paid packs list PDF, CSV and JSON. BuzzAbout's export starts at Pro. Export isn't mentioned on Karmit's or Reddinbox's pricing pages.

## What We'd Do First

Set up Reddit Pro today. It costs nothing, it has no access question, and it should show you which jobs you still miss. Confirm the details in the product, including the three-month limit, because our sources are second-hand.

Then choose by the job you miss most. For browsing communities, try Karmit's free map, knowing it's new. For themes, try Reddinbox's trial. For ranked complaints, PainOnSocial Starter is $19 a month, or IdeaFast is $19 once. Run a real question through one tool for a week before you pay for a second.

Then treat every GummySearch alternative as something you might have to leave. Keep your lists outside it. Send the four questions. Prefer monthly billing or small packs over a year up front, at least until March 2027 has passed and you can see who still reads Reddit. Reddit's own updates may change this picture, so check before you commit.

And be honest about what you opened GummySearch for. If it was studying a market, Reddix isn't your tool, and the seven above are a better place to look. If it was finding people who are asking for a product like yours, [compare Reddix with GummySearch](/alternatives/gummysearch) and try the 7-day trial.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the best GummySearch alternative?

No single tool replaces it, and Trend Seeker's blog says the same. For most founders the best GummySearch alternative is a pair: Reddit Pro for free discovery and tracking, plus one paid research tool. Karmit suits subreddit browsing, Reddinbox suits themes and questions, and PainOnSocial suits low-cost pain scans. Karmit is very new, so treat it as unproven.

### Is there a free GummySearch alternative?

Reddit Pro is the fullest free option, per TechCrunch and Practical Ecommerce, though its chart filter stops at three months. Karmit's community map and search are free, IdeaFast's first scan is free, and Adlicio's free plan gives 150 comments on a first scrape.

### Can I still recover my GummySearch data?

GummySearch's notice said all user data would be permanently deleted on December 1, 2026, and no recovery route is mentioned on its site. If you exported earlier, start from that file. If you didn't, the rebuild steps above cover your subreddit lists and themes.

### Does any GummySearch replacement hold a commercial Reddit licence?

None of the six third-party tools in this list states one. Subreddit Signals says it "runs on the official Reddit API" without claiming a licence, Redditapis says "licensed" without naming a licensor, and enterprise suites such as Meltwater announce Reddit partnerships. Reddix claims no licence either: it finds public posts through Firecrawl, a third-party search API.

### Does Reddix replace GummySearch?

Only for the lead-finding half of the job. It scores Reddit posts against your product and drafts replies you edit and post yourself. It doesn't do audience research, and it has no alerts or scheduled scans. If you used GummySearch to study a market, pick from the seven above.
