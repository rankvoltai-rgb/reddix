The best RedNudge alternative depends on why you're leaving. For faster alerts that include comments, look at Syften. To keep AI scoring and add reply drafts, Subreddit Signals. For the cheapest step up from a digest, Sublookout. For comment matches at the lowest price, RedditMentions. For drafted replies and a lead list, Reddix. For mentions across the whole web, Brand24. If a morning email at $7 a month does the job, stay on RedNudge.

No independent review of RedNudge turned up. Searches for its name on November 3, 2026 returned launch directories rewriting its description, some with wrong prices, plus RedNudge's own comparison pages. So three questions go unanswered: does a 30-minute scan plus a daily digest fit the reply window, does it watch comments, and what is its data route now that Reddit is closing public access?

We make Reddix, so this isn't independent either. RedNudge is a real rival: far cheaper than Reddix, and it scans and sends a digest without you doing anything, which Reddix doesn't. We didn't buy or run these tools. We report what their sites say, with prices checked on November 3, 2026.

## Key Takeaways

- RedNudge's pricing page lists Starter at $7 and Pro at $15 a month, with a 7-day trial and no card. Seven of its own comparison pages still say $19 for Pro.
- Its homepage says "Real-time Reddit monitoring", while scans run every 30 minutes and the main output is a daily digest.
- The 30-minute scan sits inside the timing data available. A once-a-day email often doesn't: by our arithmetic, a new post waits about 12 hours on average.
- Its homepage and privacy policy describe posts, and two comparison pages describe a post plus top comments. Whether a keyword found only in a comment triggers a match is not mentioned on its site.
- Its homepage names "Reddit's public JSON endpoints". Reports say that kind of access started returning 403 errors around May 2026. That doesn't show RedNudge is affected, so ask it.
- Stay on RedNudge if you want Reddit brand monitoring that runs untouched, can wait until morning, and want to pay $7 or $15.
- Reddix costs $45 a month, drafts three replies per thread and keeps a lead list, but it scans only when you press Scan and sends no alerts.

## Who Should Simply Stay on RedNudge

Plenty of founders should stop reading here. RedNudge beats Reddix on three things, and you should hear that from us.

- **Price.** Starter costs $84 a year and Pro $180, against $540 for Reddix Premium. Reddix's one-time $150 equals about 21 months of Starter or 10 of Pro, by our arithmetic.
- **It runs without you.** Add a keyword and it scans every 30 minutes. Reddix waits until you press Scan, and there are no scheduled scans.
- **It comes to you.** You pick the digest time, timezone and minimum score, and [Slack connects with one click](https://rednudge.com/integrations). Reddix has no email, Slack or webhook alerts.

Stay if most of these describe you:

1. **You're after Reddit brand monitoring, not a reply race.** Reading yesterday's mentions, sentiment and competitor talk is a fair use of a digest.
2. **You track a handful of terms.** Match limits work out to about 10 scored posts a day on Starter and 33 on Pro. When they run out, ["new posts stop being scored"](https://rednudge.com/pricing) until the next cycle or an upgrade.
3. **You want a small tool.** Its [RedShip comparison](https://rednudge.com/vs/redship) says "We do one job", and lists what it leaves out: long-form post drafts, a posting calendar, DM automation.
4. **You like a vendor that shows its hand.** It says F5Bot is right for some users ("we will tell you so"), and it put its data route in writing, which most rivals we read haven't.

## Does a 30-Minute Scan Plus a Daily Digest Fit the Reply Window?

Short answer: the scan does, and the email often doesn't. Slack may change that, depending on your plan.

RedNudge says two things about speed. The [homepage](https://rednudge.com/) badge reads "Real-time Reddit monitoring". The same page says "Every 30 minutes we find new posts" and that the digest "lands in your inbox every morning." Its [F5Bot comparison](https://rednudge.com/vs/f5bot) says F5Bot "wins on time-to-notification" and RedNudge on "time-to-action", because you skim one ranked email. For many people that's a good trade.

### The delay each route adds

| Route | From new post to you | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Scan alone | Up to 30 minutes, about 15 on average | "Every 30 minutes" |
| Email once a day | Up to about 24 hours, about 12 on average | Our arithmetic, if posts arrive evenly through the day |
| Slack, if set to real time | Up to 30 minutes, plus scoring time | /integrations wording; plan not stated on /pricing |

Take a 7:00 AM digest. A thread posted at 7:05 on Monday reaches your inbox around 7:00 on Tuesday. [Its support page](https://rednudge.com/support) mentions a digest frequency setting, but the public pages don't list the options.

Slack is the swing factor, and the pages differ. The homepage says you "get the same digest in your channel". [/integrations](https://rednudge.com/integrations) says "in real time, as a daily digest, or both". The [setup guide](https://rednudge.com/tools/reddit-mention-alerts-setup) says you can enable alerts for matches above a score threshold, and that "higher tiers add" Slack delivery. /pricing doesn't list Slack at all.

### What the timing studies show, and where they stop

| Source | What it covers | Figure | Limits |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [Ye et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.05328), preprint | 3,156,070 direct comments in 11 mental-health subreddits; posts under 10 comments excluded | Without official bots, 4.35% of comments arrive in 30 minutes, 7.80% in an hour, 93.47% in 24 hours. The first 30% get more upvotes than a simulation predicts | One topic. Correlation. The earliest comments scored lower on quality |
| [Goyal, Claire and Chandrasekharan, 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.14575), preprint | Reddit Answers audit: queries from 20 advice and support communities, 14.68M comments | Comments it selected came a median 1.2 hours after the post, against 5.9 hours for the rest | Observational, the authors say. Generative search, not the thread itself |
| [Graffius, 2026](https://www.scottgraffius.com/blog/files/lifespan-halflife-of-social-media-posts-update-2026.html) | Average half-life of a Reddit post | 150 minutes | Posts, not replies. One author, no Reddit sample given. Varies by subreddit |

### Our reading, which is inference

The scan fits. At worst, 30 minutes puts you in a thread when, in the Ye data, about 4% of its comments had arrived. That's well inside both the 1.2-hour and the 150-minute figures.

The once-a-day email is the weak link if you want to join early. Twelve hours is ten times that 1.2-hour median and almost five times Graffius's half-life. A post made just after your digest goes out could wait until 93% of its comments have landed, if your subreddits behave like those groups. They may not.

RedNudge offers a yardstick of its own. Its [May 27, 2026 F5Bot post](https://rednudge.com/blog/best-f5bot-alternatives-2026) says threads are "usually 20-90 minutes old" when F5Bot's email lands, and calls that "fine" for brand mentions and "marginal" for lead-gen replies. That's an unmeasured claim about a rival, but set a next-morning digest beside it and judge for yourself.

Nothing we could locate measures how fast one early reply gains visibility, or shows that replying sooner causes more of it. In Goyal's model, a comment's score within its thread predicted selection more strongly than its age. So there's no "first hour" rule here, and a good reply at hour six may well beat a poor one at minute ten. For every tool's stated speed by plan, see our [Reddit monitoring tools guide](/blog/best-reddit-monitoring-tools).

## Does RedNudge Watch Comments?

Its site points both ways, and the question that matters is not answered.

**Pages that describe posts:**

- The [homepage](https://rednudge.com/): "Every 30 minutes we find new posts."
- The [pricing FAQ](https://rednudge.com/pricing): "RedNudge monitors Reddit posts specifically".
- The [privacy policy](https://rednudge.com/privacy), updated August 26, 2026: "We only monitor publicly available Reddit posts". It says the data sent to Claude is "post titles and text".

**Pages that mention comments:**

- [/vs/redship](https://rednudge.com/vs/redship), in a row labelled comments: "Yes (post + top comments scored together)".
- [/vs/f5bot](https://rednudge.com/vs/f5bot): "Claude reads every match (post + top comments)".
- The [setup guide](https://rednudge.com/tools/reddit-mention-alerts-setup) table: "Posts + comments + thread context".

Our inference is that top comments may be read as context for a post that already matched. Whether a keyword appearing only in a comment triggers a match is not mentioned on its site.

This matters most for brands. Someone asks "what do you use for invoicing?" and a stranger answers with your product's name. The post title never mentions you. A tool that matches on posts alone wouldn't surface that thread.

There's a cheap way to check, since both trials need no card. Run RedNudge beside [Syften](https://syften.com/?redirect=false), whose site says it matches comments, on the same keywords for a week. Then see whether RedNudge also caught the threads Syften caught through a comment.

## What Is RedNudge's Data Route, Now That Reddit Is Closing Access?

RedNudge has been more open about this than most. The homepage FAQ says: "We monitor public Reddit posts using Reddit's public JSON endpoints." Its [GummySearch post](https://rednudge.com/blog/best-gummysearch-alternatives-2026), dated May 28, 2026, goes further: "we use unauthenticated polling, no Reddit OAuth, no commercial license". It calls that "not immune to future changes" and adds that "Reddit could rate-limit, paywall, or remove these endpoints at any time." Few vendors write that about themselves.

### What's been reported since

- A [write-up on DEV](https://dev.to/tonywangca/why-reddit-blocked-unauthenticated-json-in-2026-and-how-to-still-get-reddit-data-58b9), first published by Crawlora, which sells a Reddit API, says Reddit announced the end of unauthenticated `.json` access on May 28, 2026, with 403 errors within days.
- A [GitHub issue](https://github.com/kellyaa/agent-newsletter/issues/328) from an unrelated project logged a 403 on a `.json` request from a developer's machine on October 25, 2026.
- [Prowlo's blog](https://prowlo.com/blog/reddit-data-api), also from a vendor in this market, reports 403s since late May.

Reddit's own pages were blocked to our tools, so all of that is second-hand. Separately, the press reports a wider closure, announced September 30, 2026: new public API requests stopped after October 31, RSS ends November 13, access starts closing for unregistered apps on January 12, 2027, and what's left closes in March 2027, per [TechRepublic](https://www.techrepublic.com/article/news-reddit-rss-public-api-shutdown/) and [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/379892/reddit-killing-rss-feeds-public-api-access-ai-scrapers). Reports differ on registered users. [heise](https://www.heise.de/en/news/Reddit-to-shut-down-RSS-feeds-and-public-API-11473400.html) says they aren't affected, while TechRepublic says "Registration alone does not preserve" access indefinitely.

**None of this proves RedNudge is affected.** Those reports concern other people's scrapers. Its method may have changed since May, and a homepage FAQ can lag a product. We haven't seen a report of RedNudge failing, and we're not claiming one. Its site offers little to check against: /changelog returns a 404, and its newest blog post is dated June 3, 2026.

### Five questions to send its support team

1. Is the homepage line still accurate, or did your method change after May 2026?
2. Have you registered with Reddit, or answered its outreach, ahead of January 12, 2027?
3. What's your plan for March 2027? TechRepublic tells buyers to "ask vendors how they plan to maintain access".
4. If a scan comes back empty because of a block, how would I know? Your [support page](https://rednudge.com/support) says digests go out "when there are new matches", so a quiet inbox could mean either.
5. Would I get a refund if access changes mid-month?

Question four has a free partial test. Add a busy keyword during the trial and check that matches carry recent timestamps. That tells you about today, not March.

The same questions apply to everyone below, including us. Reddix finds public posts through a third-party search API (Firecrawl), not Reddit's API. We don't claim that makes it immune to Reddit's changes, and we claim no licence. The monitoring tools guide linked above compares vendors on March 2027.

### 1. Syften — best for the fastest stated alerts, comments included

Syften answers the first two questions most directly. [Its FAQ](https://syften.com/?redirect=false) says it "tracks Reddit posts and comments", and for Reddit "we aim for at most a 1-minute delay." That's a target, not a measurement. [Its docs](https://syften.com/documentation) say "We scan the entire Reddit", so you needn't list subreddits.

Entry is $29.95 a month for 3 community filters, with no Slack or AI filtering. Those start at Standard, $49.95, over three times RedNudge Pro. The [Syften alternatives guide](/blog/syften-alternatives) works through its plans.

You'd also swap a score for a rule. AI filtering is an accept-or-reject check you write yourself, and lead scoring isn't mentioned on its site. Neither is built-in drafting: "We provide the mentions and get out of the way." In return you get Hacker News, GitHub, Stack Exchange, podcasts and more, where RedNudge is Reddit-only.

On the route, its docs say it sees Reddit "from the perspective of a logged-out user". How it gets the data is not mentioned on its site, so send the questions above here too.

### 2. Subreddit Signals — best for keeping AI scores and adding drafts, on a stated API route

If you like what RedNudge's score does and want it sooner, this is the nearest match. Each post gets 1-10 on [five dimensions](https://www.subredditsignals.com/how-it-works/lead-scoring), which it calls "a prioritization score, not a conversion probability." It says [matches arrive](https://www.subredditsignals.com/reddit-keyword-alerts) "within about five minutes", down from "around 3.3 hours" on earlier polling. Both are its own figures.

Its pricing page says it "scans Reddit through the official API". A commercial licence is not mentioned on its site. You generate a draft from a Voice Profile, edit it and post from your own account; it ["does not auto-post replies."](https://www.subredditsignals.com/)

[Starter is $29 a month and Pro $59](https://www.subredditsignals.com/pricing). What else it costs you:

- **Scope.** It watches only subreddits you pick, up to 10 or 25. RedNudge can leave a keyword open to all of Reddit.
- **Tokens.** Lead Tokens are "weekly credits you spend to unlock and act on leads": 15 or 25 a week.
- **Setup.** The 14-day trial needs a card, and Slack is Pro only, by webhook.

Comments are unclear here too. One page says it scans "new posts and comments", and the keyword-alerts page describes posts.

### 3. Sublookout — best for the cheapest step up from a daily digest

[$12 a month](https://sublookout.com/pricing), $3 less than RedNudge Pro, buys a check interval "as low as 10 min" and an Instant Slack mode that messages you on each match. That's a third of RedNudge's scan gap, with alerts in place of a morning email. The limits are roomy: 10 monitors, each with 20 subreddits and 20 keywords.

[Its docs](https://sublookout.com/docs) are blunt about the trade: it "monitors posts (new submissions) only — not comments". Keywords match by simple substring, from subreddits you name, and AI scoring of matches is not mentioned on its site.

On the route, a [March 2026 post](https://sublookout.com/blog/how-to-monitor-reddit-keywords) says "the official Reddit API" and calls that access "not at risk of suddenly disappearing". That predates Reddit's September notice. Its [terms](https://sublookout.com/terms) are more careful, warning that platform changes "may affect or interrupt the service". On the trial, its pricing FAQ says "a card is required", and its [homepage](https://sublookout.com/) says "No credit card required until your trial ends."

### 4. RedditMentions — best for comment matches at the lowest price

This is the one pick cheaper than RedNudge. [€4.49 a month](https://redditmentions.com/pricing) covers 5 subreddits and 5 keywords, and €9.49 covers 15 of each. Both plans [match posts and comments](https://redditmentions.com/), and both include negative keywords and Slack.

On paper its clock looks like RedNudge's: checks "Every 30 minutes, 24/7" and a daily email. The difference is Slack. [Its guide](https://redditmentions.com/guides/slack-integration-setup) says alerts are "sent immediately" on a match, averaging about 15 minutes after a post or comment goes up, by the vendor's own sum.

The catch may be the reason you bought RedNudge. AI filtering and intent tags are not mentioned on its site, so expect every keyword match, minus your negative keywords. You also name subreddits. On the route, the homepage says "Our Reddit scraper", and API use is not mentioned on its site. The trial asks for card details, though a 5-match preview doesn't.

### 5. Reddix — best for drafted replies and a lead list, if you'll press Scan yourself

This is ours, and it's fifth for a reason: it isn't a monitor, so on its own it does nothing for the reply-window problem. It handles what comes after you spot a thread.

Reddix [scores each post 0-100 against your product](/features/intent-detection), as Hot, Warm, Research or Low. Then it [drafts three replies per thread](/features/ai-replies): Direct, Story and Expert. You edit one, click Copy & Go and post from your own account. Saved leads go in a lead list with statuses: engaged, replied, converted, archived.

RedNudge's homepage offers "AI-drafted reply guidance", and one comparison page mentions an extension that "drafts replies and DMs for you to paste manually". Neither is on its plan lists. Lead statuses, CRM features and exports are not mentioned on its site.

The limits, stated plainly:

- **Scans run when you press Scan**, at most one every 5 minutes. No scheduled scans, no background monitoring.
- **No email, Slack or webhook alerts.** If you don't open it, you hear nothing.
- **Posts, not comments**, focused on subreddits you choose from its suggestions. RedNudge can leave a keyword open to all of Reddit.
- **Reddit only.**
- **$45 a month with a 7-day trial, or $150 once.** No free plan, and three times RedNudge Pro.
- **Route:** a third-party search API (Firecrawl), not Reddit's API.

It never posts, votes or messages, and it uses no Reddit accounts. Each subreddit's rules still apply to what you post. Pick it if you'll open it most days anyway and want the writing and tracking cut down.

### 6. Brand24 — best for brand monitoring across the whole web

RedNudge says it is ["Reddit-only by design"](https://rednudge.com/vs/f5bot). If your Reddit brand monitoring is really brand monitoring, with news, reviews, TikTok and podcasts in the same view, [Brand24](https://brand24.com/prices/) is the step up. Its [Reddit page](https://brand24.com/reddit-monitoring/) refers to "public Reddit comments and posts", and AI sentiment comes on every plan.

Individual is $249 a month on monthly billing, about 36 times RedNudge Starter, and its pricing page says "Update every 12h". That's a slower stated cycle than RedNudge's 30-minute scan. Team is $349 with hourly updates, and "Realtime updates" start at Pro, $499, though the Reddit page says "Real-time monitoring of subreddits" without that limit.

This suits a brand or PR team reporting across channels. Reply drafting is not mentioned on its site. Neither is its Reddit route: RedNudge's GummySearch post says Brand24 holds a paid agreement with Reddit, but the Brand24 pages we opened don't say so. The 14-day trial needs no card.

For free alerts, [our F5Bot guide](/blog/f5bot-alternatives-reddit-monitoring) covers its plans; RedNudge itself recommends F5Bot for niche terms.

## A Week's Trial Settles More Than Any Review

Nothing here replaces a week of your own numbers, and RedNudge's no-card trial makes that cheap. No directory listing will tell you these things, and they're what should decide it.

1. **Add one busy keyword and one niche one.** The busy term shows that matches are flowing, so a quiet inbox means something.
2. **Time the digest.** For each thread in the email, subtract its post time from the arrival time. That's your real reply window, in your subreddits, and it beats every study above.
3. **Try Slack.** Find out whether real-time alerts exist on the plan you'd pay for, and whether a score threshold applies.
4. **Compare comments.** Run Syften's no-card trial beside it, and note what shows up in only one.
5. **Send the five route questions.** Keep the reply. It's worth having in writing before January 12, 2027.

If threads arrive young enough and the answers satisfy you, stay on RedNudge and keep the money. If they arrive cold, go to the pick that fits your reason. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is RedNudge really real-time?

Its homepage says "Real-time Reddit monitoring", while scans run every 30 minutes and the main output is a daily digest. Its integrations page says Slack can run "in real time", and its homepage says Slack gets "the same digest". Ask which applies to your plan before you judge it on speed.

### How much does RedNudge cost?

Starter is $7 a month and Pro is $15, per its pricing page, checked on November 3, 2026. There's a 7-day trial with no card, and no free plan is listed. Seven of its comparison pages and Product Hunt show $19 for Pro.

### Is there a free alternative to RedNudge?

F5Bot has a free plan marked ["personal use only"](https://f5bot.com/tiers). Sublookout's docs describe a free plan with 60-minute checks and no email or Slack alerts, though its pricing page doesn't mention one. The other picks here are paid after a trial or preview, and Reddix has no free plan.

### Is RedNudge good for Reddit brand monitoring?

For a morning read of mentions, sentiment and competitor talk at $7 or $15, it's a sensible fit, and brand work is more forgiving of delay than replying for leads. The open question is comment-only mentions, since those matter for brands. Test that in the trial before you rely on it.

### Can Reddix replace RedNudge?

Only for part of the job. Reddix scores posts, drafts replies and keeps a lead list, but it doesn't scan by itself or send alerts. Some founders may want both, at $52 or $60 a month. Reddix doesn't import RedNudge's matches, so that means two separate lists.

Which of the three questions decides it for you, and what did RedNudge's support say when you asked?
